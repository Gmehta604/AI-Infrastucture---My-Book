Your Path: Projects, Plan and Reading List

Knowledge becomes expertise through building. This chapter turns the book into a plan: a sequence of portfolio projects that each solve a real business bottleneck, a 12-month schedule, and a curated reading list in the order you should read it.

How to present each project

A hiring manager reads a README for two minutes. Structure each one as:

  1. Problem: one paragraph in business terms ("A company serving 5M requests/day at $X...").
  2. Baseline: what the naive solution costs or how slowly it runs, measured.
  3. What you changed: each optimisation, with the measured effect of each.
  4. Results: one chart (latency vs throughput, cost vs accuracy) and one table.
  5. Trade-offs and what you'd do next: shows judgement.
  6. Reproduce: exact commands, versions, hardware.

Where to get compute cheaply

  • Free: Google Colab, Kaggle notebooks (GPU hours per week), Lightning AI free tier.
  • Cheap hourly: RunPod, Vast.ai, Lambda, TensorDock for single GPUs; spot instances on major clouds.
  • Serverless: Modal and similar platforms often include monthly free credits.
  • Startup and research credits from cloud providers, and NVIDIA's developer programmes.
  • Always shut down instances when finished; set budget alerts.

Final key idea. The people who stand out in AI infrastructure are not the ones who have read the most, but the ones who can say: "I measured it, here is where the time and money went, here is what I changed, and here is the number that moved." Every chapter of this book gives you something to measure. Go measure it.

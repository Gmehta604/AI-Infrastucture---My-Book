Multimodal and Generative Media

Frontier models see images, read documents, listen, speak, and in many products generate images, video and music. This chapter explains the two big families (vision-language models and diffusion models) and how they are built and served.

Contrastive learning: CLIP

OpenAI's CLIP (2021) trained an image encoder and a text encoder together on 400 million image–caption pairs. The objective: matching pairs should have high dot product; mismatched pairs low. The result is a shared embedding space for images and text, enabling zero-shot classification ("a photo of a dog" vs. "a photo of a cat") and image search.

import torch, torch.nn.functional as F
def clip_loss(img_emb, txt_emb, temperature=0.07):
    img = F.normalize(img_emb, dim=-1); txt = F.normalize(txt_emb, dim=-1)
    logits = img @ txt.T / temperature                   # (N, N)
    labels = torch.arange(len(img))
    return (F.cross_entropy(logits, labels) + F.cross_entropy(logits.T, labels)) / 2

Successors: SigLIP / SigLIP 2 (Google, sigmoid loss), used as the vision tower in many open VLMs.

The dominant recipe (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InternVL, Llama 4, Gemma 3):

Training usually goes: (1) train only the projector on image-caption pairs; (2) unfreeze and train on instruction data (charts, documents, screenshots, OCR, reasoning over images). Natively multimodal models (Gemini, GPT-4o onward, Llama 4, Kimi K3) mix modalities during pretraining itself.

Infrastructure note: images are expensive. A high-resolution screenshot can become 1,000–3,000 tokens. Document-processing pipelines at scale (invoices, contracts, medical records) are large inference workloads, and image-token cost is a common optimisation target (tiling strategies, resolution limits, caching).

Speech and audio

  • ASR (speech-to-text): Whisper and successors; streaming models for real-time captions.
  • TTS (text-to-speech): neural codecs plus Transformers (ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Cartesia, open models like Kokoro, Sesame, Orpheus).
  • Speech-to-speech models (GPT-4o-style realtime voice, Gemini Live, Moshi) process audio tokens directly for sub-second, interruptible conversation.

Latency is everything in voice: humans expect a response within about 300–500 ms. Voice-agent infrastructure (WebRTC transport, streaming ASR, fast first token, streaming TTS) is a distinct engineering specialty.

Diffusion models: generating images and video

A diffusion model learns to reverse a noising process. During training, take a clean image, add a random amount of Gaussian noise, and train a network to predict the noise. At generation time, start from pure noise and remove it step by step.

import torch, torch.nn.functional as F

T = 1000
betas = torch.linspace(1e-4, 0.02, T)
alpha_bar = torch.cumprod(1 - betas, dim=0)

def diffusion_loss(model, x0, text_emb):
    t = torch.randint(0, T, (x0.shape[0],))
    noise = torch.randn_like(x0)
    a = alpha_bar[t].view(-1, 1, 1, 1)
    xt = a.sqrt() * x0 + (1 - a).sqrt() * noise          # forward (noising) process
    return F.mse_loss(model(xt, t, text_emb), noise)      # learn to predict the noise

Key developments:

Idea What it did
DDPM (2020) Made diffusion work well
Latent diffusion / Stable Diffusion (2022) Diffuse in a compressed latent space from a VAE: ~48× fewer pixels to process
Classifier-free guidance Push samples toward the text prompt; the "guidance scale" knob
Diffusion Transformers (DiT) Replace the U-Net with a Transformer; scales better (Sora, SD3, FLUX)
Flow matching / rectified flow Straighter paths from noise to data; fewer steps (SD3, FLUX)
Distillation (LCM, consistency models, adversarial distillation) 1–4 steps instead of 25–50: real-time generation
Autoregressive image generation GPT-4o-style native image output from the LLM itself (2025)

Video models (OpenAI Sora, Google Veo 3, Kling, Runway, Luma, Wan, Hunyuan) extend DiTs to spacetime patches of video latents. They are among the most compute-hungry inference workloads in existence: a few seconds of high-resolution video can take GPU-minutes.

World models and robotics

Models that predict how an environment evolves (Google's Genie 3, NVIDIA Cosmos) generate interactive worlds and synthetic training data for robots. Vision-language-action (VLA) models (Physical Intelligence's π series, Google's Gemini Robotics, NVIDIA GR00T) output robot actions. Robotics is widely considered the next frontier for foundation models, limited mainly by data.

Serving generative media

Diffusion serving differs from LLM serving: work is compute-bound (big batched convolutions/attention over latents), requests are few but heavy, and optimisations include step distillation, caching intermediate features across steps, FP8/FP4 quantization, TensorRT compilation and batching across users. Many companies' "our image feature is too expensive" problems are solved with step reduction and quantization.

Project: Document-understanding pipeline with cost control

Business problem: an insurance company receives 50,000 scanned claim documents a day and pays people to key in fields.

  1. Use an open VLM (e.g., a Qwen-VL-family model) served with vLLM, plus a specialised OCR model as a baseline.
  2. Extract fields to a JSON schema with structured (constrained) decoding.
  3. Measure accuracy per field, tokens per page, and pages per GPU-hour. Try lower image resolution and see where accuracy drops.
  4. Deliverable: cost per 1,000 pages vs. manual entry, and a confidence threshold that routes hard pages to a human.
